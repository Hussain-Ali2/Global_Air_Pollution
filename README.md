# 🌍 Global air pollution

## 📝Introduction

Air pollution is one of the biggest environmental and health issues today. Many people overlook air quality when moving or traveling, even though it directly affects health and quality of life. This project analyzes global air pollution data to find the most polluted areas, understand the links between pollutants, and highlight the need for stricter environmental rules and public awareness to ensure a cleaner, healthier future.

## ⚙️ Tools 
- **Python** – data cleaning
- **Excel** – initial data organization and preprocessing  
- **Power BI** – interactive dashboards and visual insights  

## 🎯 Objectives  
The project aims to identify countries and cities with the worst and cleanest air quality, and to estimate the number of people at risk from air pollution. It also examines the relationship between population levels and pollutant concentrations, analyzes whether there is a statistically significant correlation between CO₂, NO₂, O₃, and PM2.5, and identifies the type of pollutant that contributes most to overall air pollution.

## 📊 Key Findings  
India has the most polluted cities, while Ecuador has the cleanest areas. PM2.5 has the greatest impact on air quality. Population levels do not have a strong effect, and regions at the edges tend to have less pollution, whereas central areas are generally more polluted.  

## 💡 Recommendations  
- **Travelers** should consider air quality when choosing destinations.  
- **Governments** must enforce stricter environmental regulations to control emissions.  
- **Factories** should avoid using harmful particles and using green particles to reduce pollution.
